# Classroom360

Web console and shared backend for *Classroom360: A Unified Architecture of AI Scheduling, Smart Key Lockers, and CCTV Monitoring for Educational Institutions*.

```
classroom360/
  supabase/
    migrations/   Database schema, security (RLS), approval workflow, locker functions, saving generated schedules,
                  instructor work hours, school hours, lecture and lab units, teaching-load import,
                  reopening a schedule, room types, classes (sections), overriding a locked meeting, pinned meetings,
                  saving several programs at once, deleting rooms and courses, key cards, locker door setup,
                  classroom cameras and the presence verdict (key + camera),
                  the locker station's card tap (one reader, four doors),
                  the evaluation trial log (confusion matrix data), a log of every scheduling run, the locker station's
                  heartbeat and tap times, and the alerts list
    seed.sql      Demo data: one college, LRC building 3rd floor (4 rooms, one 4-door key locker), 6 fictional instructors (one part-time), 15 published classes
  web/            React + TypeScript web app (admins, deans, program chairs, instructors)
    src/lib/scheduler/   The scheduling AI (runs in the browser; see "Generate schedule" below)
    scheduler-tests/     Tests and a benchmark for it
    src/lib/import/      Reads the Excel or CSV files (teaching load, rooms, course catalog, classes) and the work-hours text ("Mon-Fri 8-5")
    tests/               Tests for the import and for the room-type and meeting-plan rules, with sample Excel and CSV files,
                         and for the person-counting maths and smoothing
    src/lib/presence/    The camera feature: live picture (WebRTC), the person detector (YOLOX in a background worker), smoothing
    src/lib/evaluation/  The Chapter 2 maths (confusion matrix, precision, recall, F1, latency mean / SD / 95th percentile,
                         solver run statistics) and the CSV files of the Evaluation page
    scripts/             setup-presence.mjs downloads the person-detection model
  pi/camera-gateway/     The parent Raspberry Pi: MediaMTX relay, the access checker, installer, service files, tests
  pi/locker-station/     The locker's Raspberry Pi: card reader, four door locks, key switches, display; installer, service file, tests
  mobile/                The Flutter phone app for deans and administrators: rooms, requests, live cameras (see mobile/README.md)
```

The Flutter app (`mobile/`) talks to the **same Supabase project**. Nothing in `web/` is needed by it; everything it needs is in `supabase/`. The phone app is kept in its own Git repository, and this one ignores the `mobile/` folder; clone it into `mobile/` if you want both side by side.

## How the pieces connect

```
 Web app (React)  ─┐
 Flutter app      ─┼──►  Supabase  (Postgres + Auth + Realtime + REST)
 Locker firmware  ─┤        ▲
 Scheduler service┤        │ service_role only
 Camera service   ─┘
```

All business rules live in the database, so every client gets them for free:

- **No double bookings, across departments.** Three exclusion constraints refuse a room, instructor or student cohort booked twice at the same time, even if a client has a bug. They look at the whole institution, so one department can never take a room another department holds. `schedule_busy` shows a program chair what is already taken (room, time and owning department only), so the editor can warn before a save is refused.
- **Row Level Security** on every table, scoped by tenant and role. An instructor only sees their own published classes; a program chair only sees programs in their department.
- **Approval workflow** through one function, `set_schedule_status`: draft, submitted, approved, published, with returns (shown as "Rejected" in the app, with the dean's note). Only `published` classes open a locker.
- **Room requests.** An instructor asks for a room that is free (`free_rooms`, `create_room_request`); a dean or administrator accepts or rejects it (`decide_room_request`). An approved request opens that room's locker door for that instructor during the approved time, and takes the room off the free list.
- **A four-door locker.** One locker station is one cabinet with doors 1 to 4, each holding the key to one room (the LRC 3rd floor has four rooms). The database refuses a door number outside 1 to 4.
- **Generated schedules are saved in one step.** `apply_generated_schedule` replaces a program's draft and rejected meetings with the generated ones in a single transaction, so a failed save never leaves half a timetable. It re-checks that every meeting belongs to the program, that rooms are in use and times make sense, and the three exclusion constraints above still refuse any clash, including one another department created a moment ago.
- **School hours and instructor hours are enforced by the database, not only by the scheduler.** Each weekday has opening and closing times (a day with no row is closed). A class meeting, or a room request, outside them is refused with "outside school hours", whoever or whatever tries to save it, so a bug in any app cannot put a class at 6 AM or on a Sunday.
- **Importing a term's teaching load** goes through one function, `import_teaching_load`. Called as a dry run it reports exactly what would be created or changed and saves nothing; called to apply, it saves everything or, if any row has an error, nothing.
- **A locked meeting can still be moved, but only with a reason.** A dean or administrator can move one submitted, approved or published meeting with `override_schedule_entry` (new room, day and time, and a justification of at least a few words). The database checks the same rules as any move (no double booking, school hours), keeps the meeting's status, marks it `is_override`, writes the reason to the audit log (`SCHEDULE_OVERRIDE`, with the old and the new place) and returns warnings that do not stop it (the new room is too small or lacks the room type the class needs, the time is outside the instructor's work hours or marked unavailable by them, or a room request is waiting for that room and time). Everything else on the timetable stays as it is. `schedule_overrides` lists them for the web app. A locked meeting cannot be changed any other way except Reopen for editing.
- **Pinned meetings.** A chair can pin a draft or rejected meeting ("Keep this meeting where it is"). Generating again keeps pinned meetings exactly where they are and plans the rest around them. A pinned meeting still obeys every rule and is replaced only if the chair unpins it.
- **Several programs in one save.** `apply_generated_schedules` saves the plans of several programs in one transaction (all of them or none), so two programs planned together cannot take the same room or instructor.
- **Deleting is safe.** `delete_room` and `delete_course` refuse while anything depends on the room or course (class meetings, room requests, a locker door, classes) and say what. `set_locker_doors` and `assign_card` do the locker setup in one step with the same care: two keys can swap doors in one save and every door keeps its identity and access history; a door is switched off rather than removed; a room's key can be in one door only; one card per instructor; a card cannot belong to two people; card numbers are stored as capital letters and digits only.
- **Locker decisions** are made in the database (`authorize_key_request`, `record_key_return`), with a grace window around the class time.

## Run the web app

You need Node 22 or newer and a Supabase project (the free tier is enough).

1. **Create the database.** In the Supabase SQL editor, run these in order:
   1. `supabase/migrations/20261102000001_core_schema.sql`
   2. `supabase/migrations/20261102000002_security_and_workflow.sql`
   3. `supabase/migrations/20261103000003_four_door_locker.sql`
   4. `supabase/migrations/20261103000004_cross_department_and_requests.sql`
   5. `supabase/migrations/20261103000005_generated_schedules.sql`
   6. `supabase/migrations/20261103000006_work_hours_school_hours_and_import.sql`
   7. `supabase/migrations/20261103000007_reopen_rooms_courses_classes.sql`
   8. `supabase/migrations/20261103000008_overrides_pins_multi_program_deletes.sql`
   9. `supabase/migrations/20261104000009_cameras_and_presence.sql`
   10. `supabase/migrations/20261105000010_station_card_tap.sql`
   11. `supabase/migrations/20261106000011_evaluation_alerts_station_health.sql`
   12. `supabase/migrations/20261107000012_notifications_and_trial_capture.sql`
   13. `supabase/seed.sql` (optional demo data: the LRC 3rd floor with 4 rooms and a 4-door locker, plus three sample room requests. Run it after file 12.)

   Or with the Supabase CLI: `supabase link`, then `supabase db push` (the seed is a separate step).

   **Already ran files 1 to 11?** Run only file 12. It adds, and changes no existing data: the `notifications` inbox (each person reads only their own lines) with the database triggers that fill it when a room request is made or decided and when a timetable is submitted, rejected, reopened or published; `sweep_my_alerts`, which turns the alerts into messages (a key not returned, a silent locker station, repeated denied taps, a room with the key in the wrong hands); `mark_notifications_read`; and, for the Evaluation page, `trial_candidates`, `capture_trial` and `dismiss_trial_candidates` with the `trial_dismissals` table and two link columns on `test_trials`. `ops_alerts()` keeps its name and its columns (its logic moves to a private function that the sweep shares). The web app keeps working before you run it: the bell is simply not shown and the Evaluation page says what to run. To have the database check the alerts every minute even when nobody has a screen open, turn on the `pg_cron` extension (Database, Extensions) and run `select private.schedule_alert_sweep();` once; the file tries this itself and prints a notice if it cannot. Without it, alerts become messages while a web or phone screen of an administrator or dean is open, which is how most days will run anyway.

   **Already ran files 1 to 10?** Run files 11 and 12 (12 is described just above). File 11 adds, and changes no existing data: the `test_trials` table and its two functions (`record_test_trial`, `delete_test_trial`) for the Evaluation page; a `kind` column on `solve_jobs` (every existing row stays `saved`) and `record_solve_run`, so each run of the Generate dialog is written down; `record_tap_latency` and `station_heartbeat` plus the `station_heartbeats` table and `station_status` view, which the locker station calls (service role only); and `ops_alerts()`, the alert list for administrators and deans. The web app keeps working before you run it: the new pages say what to run, and the Dashboard simply shows no alerts card. Then update the locker station's program (`pi/locker-station`, version 1.1.0), which starts sending a heartbeat and the time each tap took; a station that is not updated keeps working but shows as "not connected yet".

   **Already ran files 1 to 9?** Run files 10 to 12. File 10 adds one function, `authorize_card_at_station`, which the locker station calls when someone taps a card: it picks the door for that instructor's class (so one card reader can serve four doors), asks `authorize_key_request` about that door, and recognises a tap by the holder of a key as a return. It can be called by the locker station only (`service_role`), changes no data, and nothing in the web app needs to be restarted. Then set up the locker's Raspberry Pi: see `pi/locker-station/README.md`. Do not run the seed again unless you want to throw away the demo schedules.

   **Already ran files 1 to 8?** Run files 9 to 12. File 9 adds a `cameras` table, a `person_count` column on the camera readings, the function `report_occupancy` and two new columns on the live room status (`person_count`, `presence`); it changes no data. Then run `npm install` once more (it adds `onnxruntime-web`, the engine that counts people in the browser) and start `npm run dev` again: the first start downloads the 20 MB person-detection model (it is checked against a checksum; `npm run setup:presence` does the same by hand). Do not run the seed again unless you want to throw away the demo schedules.

   **Already ran files 1 to 7?** Run files 8 to 12 (then `npm install` as above). File 8 is safe on top of them: it adds two columns to the meetings table (`is_override` and `is_pinned`, both false), and new functions. It does not touch your data (card numbers already stored stay as they are; cards given from now on are saved in capitals without spaces or colons, and the locker station must send them that way). Stop `npm run dev` and start it again. Do not run the seed again unless you want to throw away the demo schedules.

   **Already ran files 1 to 6?** Run files 7 to 12. File 7 changes no data except tidying the spelling of room types ("Chemistry Laboratory" becomes `chemistry_lab`). Same advice: `npm install`, restart `npm run dev`, and do not run the seed again unless you want to throw away the demo schedules.

   **Already ran files 1 to 5 from an earlier delivery?** Run files 6 to 12 (file 6 is safe on top of them: existing instructors become full-time with no work-hour limits, and the school opens Monday to Saturday in the hours it already had), then `npm install` again because the importer adds two packages (`papaparse` for CSV and `read-excel-file` for Excel). Do not run the seed again unless you want to throw away the demo schedules: it rebuilds the demo institution from nothing.

   **Already ran files 1 to 4?** Run files 5 to 12, then `npm install`.

   **Already ran the first two migrations and the old nine-door seed?** Run files 3 to 12, then `supabase/seed.sql` again. The seed rebuilds the demo data and keeps accounts that have already signed up, with their roles. It is safe to run as often as you like. (Did you already run the earlier version of file 3? Run file 4 and the seed; you do not need file 3 again.)
2. **Turn off public sign-ups.** Authentication, Providers, Email: disable "Allow new users to sign up". Accounts are created by an admin (Authentication, Users, Add user).
3. **Configure the app.**
   ```
   cd web
   cp .env.example .env.local
   ```
   Fill in `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` from Project Settings, API. The URL is only the address, like `https://abcdefgh.supabase.co`, with nothing after `.co` (not `/rest/v1/`). Use the anon key only. The `service_role` key must never go in the web app or the mobile app. After changing `.env.local`, stop `npm run dev` and start it again.
4. **Install and start.**
   ```
   npm install
   npm run dev
   ```
5. **Create the first admin.** Add yourself in Authentication, Users, sign in once, then in the SQL editor:
   ```sql
   update profiles set role = 'admin' where email = 'you@example.edu';
   ```
   From then on, roles and departments are managed in the app under **Users**.
   New accounts start as `instructor`. If the email matches an instructor record, the account is linked to it automatically.

Useful commands: `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`, `npm test` (the scheduler's tests, the importer's tests and the class helpers' tests; `npm run test:scheduler` and `npm run test:import` run parts of it. Node 22.18 or newer; they run without a database).

## Moving around the web app

The look is the same on every page (navy and brass, the brass key tag for "a key is out", IBM Plex with a condensed face for titles and figures); these are the ways of getting around that every page shares.

- **Jump to a page.** Press **Ctrl K** (Cmd K on a Mac), or press the **Jump to a page** button above the menu, type a few letters of what you want ("audit", "key", "print") and press Enter. The list only offers the pages your role may open, and Esc closes it without going anywhere.
- **Search.** The long lists have a search box: Rooms, Instructors, Courses (the classes and the catalog separately), Users, Audit log, Lockers (key cards), Departments. Every word you type has to match, in any order, ignoring capitals and accents ("science lab" finds the Science Hall's Chemistry Lab). Press **/** anywhere on the page to go to the search box and **Esc** to empty it. A line under the box says how many of the total are shown, and an empty result offers **Clear search and filters**.
- **Filter chips and sorting.** The chips above a list (for example All, In service, Out of service) show how many each one holds and say when they are pressed. Press a column heading to sort by it, press it again to reverse; a small arrow and the heading's `aria-sort` say which column is sorted.
- **Campus overview.** The four figures at the top are on a dark **key ledger** plate, set in flap digits that turn over when a number changes, with a row of lamps under each (rooms in use, keys out). They are still links: Rooms in use filters the board, Keys out and Keys not returned open Lockers, Alerts scrolls to the alerts; a line at the foot of the plate says who tapped last and whether they were let in. Under the alerts, **Today on the clock** draws every room across the school day on one strip: a block for each published class, grey when it is over, brass when it is in session (red when the room needs a look), outlined when it is still to come, hatched hours when the school is closed, and a brass line with the time at the moment it is now. The rooms below can be shown as a **board** of key tags (a brass tag is a key that is out, an ink tag a key in its locker, a red edge a room to look at), as **floors** (each building with a dark plate per floor, top floor first, the same tags on it) or as a **table**. Press a tag to see why it is flagged, the room and the camera's last report, with links to the lockers and cameras. The view and **All / In use / Needs a look** are remembered in this browser.
- **Your day (program chairs and instructors).** The class in session is marked **Now**, the next one **Next**, and classes already over are shown quieter.
- **The school clock.** A small panel at the top of the menu shows the time on the school's clock, whether the school is open and until when (or when it opens next), a bar for the day with a marker for now, and, for administrators, deans and program chairs, how many keys are out and how many were not returned. On a short screen the key counts are left out so the whole menu still fits; the menu fades at its foot when it scrolls.
- **Facts under each title.** Most pages open with a short row of figures in the page's own words (Rooms: rooms, seats in all, in service, buildings; Users: people and how many hold each role; Schedules: meetings, rooms used, instructors teaching, published; and so on). They are plain text, not buttons.
- **Rooms layout.** Rooms can be shown as a **List** or as a **Plan**: the same floors as on the overview, each room a tile with its key tag, its seats against the largest room, and its room types; a room that is out of service has a dashed outline. Press a tile to edit the room. The choice is remembered.
- **Sign-in page and empty lists.** The sign-in page draws the key rack the system is built around: four hooks, a tag on each, and the key to room 303 out. An empty list shows a small drawing from the same set (an empty hook, an empty week, a blank sheet) with what to do next.
- **Schedules.** The status chips under the title (Draft, Awaiting approval, Approved, Published, Rejected; only those that have meetings) are switches: press one to see only those meetings on the grid, press it again to see every status. They are off while you are editing. The grid marks today's column and draws a line at the current time, on the school's clock. Hovering or tabbing to a meeting highlights the other meetings of the same course.
- **Lockers.** A door shows a lamp (dark when the key is in, brass when it is out, a pulsing red when it was not returned) and how long the key has been out ("Out for 35 min").
- **School hours** shows the week as you change it, with the closing-time check beside the field, and says when changes are not saved yet (**Undo changes** puts them back). **Timetables:** the left and right arrow keys turn the page. **Cameras:** the options are switches, and **Show cameras** can narrow the list to the ones that need a look.
- **Messages and errors.** A notice can be dismissed, a failed list has **Try again**, and a list that is loading shows a grey outline of itself instead of a bare "Loading".
- **Motion.** Pages fade in, key tags and notices ease in, and a loading list shimmers. For a person whose system asks for reduced motion (the "reduce motion" setting) all of this is switched off, and the focus ring (a brass outline) stays on every control for keyboard use.
- **The tab title** names the page ("Rooms · Classroom360") and shows the number of unread messages in front when there are some ("(3) Rooms · Classroom360").

## Timetables, notifications and capturing trials

**Printing a timetable.** Open **Timetables** (or press **Print or export** on Schedules). Choose the term, what each page is for (student block, instructor or room), a program if you want only one, and which page to show; **All** prints every page, one per sheet. Press **Print or save PDF**, then pick "Save as PDF" as the printer to get a file; the suggested file name already says what it is (for example `classroom360-timetable-block-ay-2026-2027-1st-semester-all`). A page that only has published meetings says "Final: all meetings are published". The student block is the program, the year and the block letter (for example BSCPE 4A), which is how the database keeps two groups from sharing a time. A browser print preview is the only thing that was not run on a real printer here: the PDF was made with Chrome's own print engine and read back (one landscape page for each block, no menu and no buttons on it).

**Notifications.** The bell in the menu (and in the top bar on a phone-width screen) shows how many messages are unread, and the tab's title carries the number too, so a hidden tab shows that something is waiting. Who is told what:

| What happened | Who gets a message |
| --- | --- |
| An instructor makes a room request | every administrator and dean |
| A request is approved or rejected | the instructor who asked (a rejection's reason is in the message) |
| A chair submits a timetable | every administrator and dean |
| A timetable is rejected, reopened or published | the chairs of that program's department; when it is published also the instructors who teach in it and have a login |
| A key is not returned after its class, a locker station stops reporting, a room has its key with someone who is not scheduled there, or a card is denied three or more times (an unknown card, a deactivated one, or one booked in a different room) | every administrator and dean (once for each occurrence, so a key that stays out is not announced again every minute; repeated denied taps are told at most once a day for each card and reason) |

The person who did the thing is never told about it. Alerts that are only information ("teacher not here" and the like) stay on the dashboard and are not turned into messages. Clicking a message marks it read and opens the page it is about; **Mark all read** clears them. The database cleans up read messages after 30 days and everything after 90.

How the alerts get in: an alert has no event of its own (a key simply stays out; a station simply goes quiet), so an administrator's or dean's open screen asks the database once a minute to turn the alerts into messages (the database lets only one such check through every 20 seconds). If `pg_cron` is switched on (see the migration notes), the database does it every minute by itself.

**Pop-ups on the computer.** The panel has a switch, "Get a pop-up on this computer when a message arrives". It uses the browser's own notifications and works while the web app is open in a tab, even a hidden one, and only for a tab that is hidden (when you are looking at the page, the bell is enough). The phone app shows a banner while it is open and a number on its bell.

**What is not built:** a message to a phone or computer that has the app closed. That needs a push service (Firebase Cloud Messaging for Android; iPhones need Apple's push service, which a free Apple developer account cannot use), a server-side sender and a stored device token for each phone, and it is a separate piece of work. There is no email either. Until then, a closed app shows the messages the next time it is opened.

**Capturing trials from the log.** On the Evaluation page, **Capture from the log** lists the card taps, the door closings and the latest camera report of each room from the last few hours that are not yet a trial. For each line you choose what was really true (the test condition you set up: for a card trial "Authorized instructor within scheduled time", "Unregistered or invalid credential" and so on), see how it will count (TP, FP, TN or FN) and press **Record**. The system's own answer (granted or denied, key detected or not, occupied or vacant), the time and, for a card, the response time are copied from the log, so they cannot be mistyped or flattered. Nothing is chosen for you, on purpose: a trial whose truth was guessed would measure nothing. **Not a test** hides a line that was not part of the evaluation (a lesson held for real, someone just testing the reader); it will not be offered again. Each tap, door closing or camera report can become one trial only, and a denied tap shows no room or door because the locker releases nothing for it. The observer's name is remembered in the browser. You can still **Record a trial by hand** for anything the log does not show.

## Generate schedule (the scheduling AI)

The scheduler is **Google OR-Tools CP-SAT**, a constraint solver. It is not a machine-learning model, so there is nothing to train: it searches for a timetable that breaks none of the hard rules and scores the rest by preference. It runs **in the chair's browser** (a Web Worker, so the page stays responsive), never on the Raspberry Pi and never on a server.

- **Hard rules, never broken:** a room, an instructor or a student block is never in two places at once, counting every other department's bookings (`schedule_busy`) as fixed; the room must seat the class and have the facility the course needs; instructors' work hours, unavailable times and weekly hour limits are respected; one meeting of a section per day; classes stay inside the school hours of each day (Monday to Saturday, 7:00 AM to 9:00 PM, until the administrator sets them). Meetings the program already has submitted, approved or published stay exactly where they are.
- **Preferences, traded off:** earlier in the day, avoid evenings, keep a section in one room, spread a section's meetings over non-adjacent days, fewer teaching days per instructor (much more so for part-time instructors), and keep special rooms free of ordinary lectures.
- **How it works:** a fast first-fit plan in milliseconds, polished by a local search, then CP-SAT tries for a better or complete plan within the time you choose (10, 30 or 90 seconds). The best plan is checked again by a separate verifier before it is shown. A plan with any violation is never offered; if CP-SAT cannot start, the quick plan is used and says so.
- **Not everything fits sometimes.** Anything that cannot be placed (for example no room seats 99 students) is listed with the reason, and you can deal with it right in the review window (see "The review window" below) or later in **Edit timetable**.
- **Several programs at once.** Tick more than one program in the dialog and they are planned in one problem, so they share rooms and instructors without clashes instead of the second program getting what the first left over. They are saved together in one transaction.
- **Pinned meetings stay.** Draft or rejected meetings the chair pinned are treated like submitted ones: kept where they are, counted against the instructor's weekly limit, with the new plan built around them.
- **Saving** replaces the program's draft and rejected meetings (except pinned ones) and keeps the rest, in one transaction. The result is ordinary drafts: drag to adjust, then submit to the dean as before.
- CP-SAT is the single-thread WebAssembly build, so it needs no special server headers (the multi-thread build would break cross-origin camera streams). It downloads (about 2 MB compressed) only when Generate runs. A typical program of up to about 45 sections has a valid plan within a second or two and then improves.
- Classes of 75 minutes are placed as 90 (the grid is 30 minutes).
- The dialog shows the school hours it will use, and warns before you start when a section cannot be placed at all (an instructor whose limit is lower than their load, a room type nobody has, no room big enough).

**Minimizing.** The Generate window is not tied to the Schedules page. A click outside it, Escape or the **Minimize** button only hides it: a run keeps going, and a plan that is waiting to be saved is kept. An **Ongoing scheduling** button then stays at the bottom right of every page (it turns into **Schedule ready to review** when the run finishes) and brings the window back, also from another page. Only **Discard** (it asks first), **Try again**, or a successful **Save as drafts** ends a session. Closing or reloading the browser tab while a run or an unsaved plan exists makes the browser ask first. In the setup stage (nothing started yet) Cancel, a click outside and Escape just close it.

**The review window.** The finished plan is shown one room at a time: a tab for each room (with its number of meetings) and that room's week below it, so you read one room instead of one huge timetable. Beside it is the **Not placed** list, one block for each meeting the scheduler could not place, with the reason and the rooms that would fit. You can:
- **drag** a block from the list onto the timetable of the room shown, or drag a placed block to move it; while you carry it the target is green when it is allowed and red when it is not, with the reason beside the pointer (Escape drops it);
- **click** any block to choose its room, day and start time from lists (the reasons update as you choose), or press **Find a free time**, or **Take off the timetable**;
- **Undo** each change, or go **Back to the generated plan** (that can be undone too). Blocks you placed or moved are brass, and a dot on a room's tab shows it has some.

Every change is checked against the same hard rules as the scheduler (room size and type, school hours, a free room, instructor and student block including other departments' bookings, work hours and weekly limit, one meeting of a class per day). A change that would break one is refused with the reasons and nothing changes, so an edited plan is as safe to save as the generated one; it is checked again by the independent verifier and by the database when saved. The run that is written down for the Evaluation page is always the scheduler's own answer, before any change by hand. The rules for this live in `src/lib/scheduler/edit.ts` and the screen in `src/components/PlanEditor.tsx`; the window itself is `src/components/GenerateHost.tsx`, mounted once in `AppShell` (the Schedules page opens it, and hears about a save, through `src/lib/generateBus.ts`).

## Instructors, school hours and the teaching-load import

**Work hours.** Every instructor is full-time or part-time and has a weekly teaching limit (default 24 hours full-time, 12 part-time; editable per person). An instructor can also have work hours: the periods on each weekday when they are on campus. The scheduler only places their classes inside those periods. An instructor with no work hours is treated as available whenever the school is open. Part-time instructors are also packed onto fewer days, because they are on campus less. A class that does not fit an instructor's hours is listed as not placed, with the reason, instead of being forced in.

**School hours.** The administrator sets when the school is open on each weekday (**School hours** page). The scheduler never places a class outside them, and the database refuses one that is. Changing the hours does not move classes already on a timetable; the page tells you how many now fall outside, and you generate that schedule again.

**Units and hours.** 1 lecture unit = 1 hour a week. 1 laboratory unit = 3 hours a week. A course with 3 lecture units and 1 lab unit becomes two sections for the scheduler: the lecture part (2 meetings of 90 minutes) and the laboratory part (one 3-hour meeting, in a room of the right type). Lecture hours are split into meetings like this: up to 1.5 hours is one meeting, up to 4 hours is two, more is three. A fractional lab unit rounds up to a whole 3-hour meeting.

**Special rooms.** A course can name a room type ("Chemistry Laboratory", "Computer Lab", "Gym", ...). When it has lab units, the room type applies to the laboratory part and the lecture part can use any room; when it has none, the room type applies to every meeting. Types are matched loosely ("Chemistry Laboratory", "chemistry lab" and "chemistry_lab" are the same). A room has a type when its `facilities` list contains it. To add a chemistry laboratory, an administrator opens **Rooms**, chooses **Add room** and ticks "Chemistry lab" (or adds their own type). The database tidies the spelling of room types on every save, so a type typed by hand always matches. The import warns you when a course asks for a type that no room has. Lectures stay out of special rooms when another room is free, so laboratories are kept for laboratory classes.

**Import teaching load.** On **Instructors**, choose **Import teaching load**, pick the term's Excel (`.xlsx`, first sheet) or CSV file, and read the preview. Nothing is saved until you press Import. If any row has an error, nothing is saved at all, and the errors are listed by row. The first row that has column headings is found automatically, a title above them is fine, and the headings are matched loosely. Columns (only the instructor, course code, program, year and section are essential):

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Instructor, Employee No, Email | The person. Matched to an existing instructor by employee number, then email, then name; otherwise created. |
| Employment | Full-time or Part-time. Left blank, the existing value is kept (new instructors are full-time). |
| Max Weekly Hours | Teaching limit. Left blank, the existing value or the default is used. |
| Work Hours | For example `Mon-Fri 8:00-17:00`, `Tue/Thu 13:00-18:00; Sat 8-12`, `MWF 9-12, 1-5`. Left blank, the existing hours are kept. |
| Course Code, Course Title | The course. Created if new. |
| Lecture Units, Lab Units | Two columns, or one `Lec/Lab` column holding `2/1`. A single `Units` column counts everything as lecture units (the dialog says so). |
| Room Type | For example `Computer Laboratory`. |
| Program, Year, Section | Which program takes it, for example `BSIT`, `2` (or `2nd`), `A` (or one column `2A`). Programs must already exist under Departments. |
| Students | Class size; used to choose a room that seats them. |

Tick the option in the dialog to read a blank instructor cell as "same instructor as the row above". A sample file can be downloaded from the dialog. Importing the same file twice creates no duplicates: the second time it finds the same instructors, courses and sections and updates them. Imported sections are new sections of the current term; run **Generate schedule** afterwards to place them.

## Adding rooms, courses and classes from a CSV file

Three pages take a CSV (or Excel `.xlsx`, first sheet) file for quick addition of many entries. All three work the same way: choose **Import CSV**, pick the file, read the preview, then press **Import**. Nothing is saved until you press it, a file with any error saves nothing at all, and the errors are listed by line (the number is the line as you see it in Excel or a text editor, blank lines included). Each dialog has **Download a template** and a "Which columns does the file need?" help. Column titles are matched loosely ("Capacity", "Seats" and "No. of seats" are the same column), a title above the column titles is fine, and a comma-, semicolon- or tab-separated file is detected.

| Page | Who | Columns (required in bold) | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Rooms**, Import CSV | Administrator | **Room code**, **Seats**, Building, Floor, Name, Room types, In service | Adds the rooms in one insert, all or none. Rooms already in the system (same code, any case) are left out and listed, never changed. |
| **Courses and classes**, Course catalog, Import CSV | Administrator, dean, program chair | **Course code**, **Course title**, **Lecture units / Lab units** (or `Lec/Lab` such as `2/1`, or one `Units`), Department, Room type | Adds the courses in one insert, all or none. Courses already in the catalog are left out and listed, never changed. |
| **Courses and classes**, Classes, Import CSV | Administrator, dean, program chair | **Course code**, **Program**, **Year and Section** (two columns, or one such as `3A`), **units**, Course title, Room type, Students, Instructor | Adds one class per line for the active term (and any course the catalog lacks), using the same database function as the teaching-load import. |

Details worth knowing:

- **Rooms.** The building is the code or name of one that exists; a line with none goes in the building chosen at the top of the dialog (a lone building is chosen for you). An unknown building is an error that lists the real ones: buildings are never created from a file, because one misspelling would create a second building. Floor accepts `3`, `3rd floor`, `3F`; "Ground" counts as 1 and `B1` as -1; without a Floor column every room is on floor 1 (the dialog says so). Without a Name the room code is used. Room types are written like `Computer Laboratory; Electronics Laboratory` and stored in the database spelling (`computer_lab`, `electronics_lab`); `Lecture room`, `Standard` or blank means an ordinary classroom. The same limits as Add room apply: seats 1 to 2000, whole numbers, at most 1000 rooms a file.
- **Course catalog.** The same limits as Add course apply: each of lecture and lab units from 0 to 12 with one decimal at most, more than 0 in total. The department is a code or name; a line with none goes in the department chosen at the top. A **program chair** can add courses to their own department only, which is also what the database enforces, so a line naming another department is an error for them. A room type that no room has yet is a warning (the course is added, but cannot be placed until a room with that type exists).
- **Classes.** This import never creates an instructor. Each name (or Employee no. or Email, to tell two people with one name apart) is matched with the instructors already in the system, the way the teaching-load import matches them; a name that is not found, an inactive instructor, or two people with the same name give a Check note, once per name, and those classes are imported without an instructor so they can be given one with **Edit**. Instructor columns such as Employment and Work hours are ignored here (use **Import from file** on **Instructors** for those). A course already in the catalog is matched by its code, and if the file gives different units or a different room type the course is updated; the preview shows "Courses: N new, M changed" before you commit. Run **Generate schedule** afterwards to place the new classes.

## Cameras and the presence check

How a room is judged, **key first, camera second**:

| Class now? | Key | Camera | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| no | | nobody / people | No class / Occupied, no class (alert) |
| yes | taken by the scheduled instructor | people | Teacher present |
| yes | taken by the scheduled instructor | nobody | Key out, room empty (alert) |
| yes | taken by the scheduled instructor | no recent reading | Key out |
| yes | held by someone else | any | Key with someone else (alert) |
| yes | in the locker | people | People, key not taken (alert) |
| yes | in the locker | nobody or no recent reading, inside the grace period | Waiting for teacher |
| yes | in the locker | nobody or no recent reading, grace period over | Teacher not here (alert) |
| yes | the room has no locker door | people / nobody / no recent reading | People seen / Room empty (alert) / Not checked |

The grace period is the school's `grace_minutes` setting. The verdict is computed in the database (view `room_status_now`, column `presence`), so the web app, the Flutter app and any report agree. A camera reading counts for 10 minutes.

**How the counting works.**
- The picture comes from the parent Raspberry Pi (`pi/camera-gateway/`) over WebRTC. The Pi checks that the person watching is signed in as an administrator or dean, by asking Supabase with that person's own token, before it sends any video.
- The page runs a small person detector (YOLOX-Tiny, Apache-2.0, `onnxruntime-web` in a background worker) on a frame every 3 seconds. It counts people. **It does not look at faces and cannot tell people apart.** The picture never leaves the device and nothing is recorded; only the room, the number of people and a confidence are sent (`report_occupancy`). A change (empty to occupied or back) is sent at once; an unchanged state at most once a minute. The last four looks are combined (at least two must see someone), so one bad frame does not flip a room.
- It runs only while the Cameras page is open and visible, in a browser on some computer or phone. Close the page and counting stops (after 10 minutes the verdict rests on the key alone: "Key out", "Waiting for teacher", "Teacher not here" or "Not checked"). With two cameras in one room, only the first counts.
- Run `npm run setup:presence` once if the page says the model is missing. Set `VITE_PRESENCE_MODEL_URL` to use a model hosted elsewhere.

**Limits to know about.**
- A person detector counts anyone, including a student or a cleaner, so "people seen" says someone is in the room. "Teacher present" additionally needs the scheduled instructor's key to be out. It is the pair, not the camera alone, that makes the verdict.
- The browser plays H.264 only on most computers. A camera set to H.265/HEVC must be switched to H.264 for the live picture to show.
- A page served over **https** cannot talk to an **http** gateway on another computer. Use the app over http on the school network, or put the gateway behind https (see `pi/camera-gateway/README.md`).
- The camera passwords live only in the Pi's settings file, never in the database.
- This has been tested with a real MediaMTX relay, the real access checker, a live H.264 stream and the real model in desktop Chrome. It has not been tried with a physical CCTV camera or on a Raspberry Pi, so test those first.

**Set up the parent Raspberry Pi:** see `pi/camera-gateway/README.md`.

## The locker station

The program on the locker's own Raspberry Pi is in `pi/locker-station/` (setup, wiring, settings and the safety rules are in its README). In short: one card reader serves all four doors. A tap goes to the database function `authorize_card_at_station`, which picks the door of the room the instructor is booked into now, logs one line, and answers; the station unlocks only that door for five seconds. A tap by someone holding a key opens that key's door so it can be put back; the key counts as returned only when its micro-switch feels it. If the database cannot be reached, nothing opens, and keys put back meanwhile are sent later with the time they really went back. Since version 1.1.0 the station also reports "I am alive" every minute (the dashboard shows it online, or raises an alert after three minutes of silence) and the time each tap took, from the card number arriving to the door unlocking or the refusal showing, which fills the response-time figures on the Evaluation page.

## Handoff notes for the Flutter app

The app is in `mobile/` (see its README). These notes list the database calls it uses and are the reference for anyone extending it. Use the same project, not a second backend.

- **Package:** `supabase_flutter`. Initialise with the same project URL and anon key.
- **Sign-in:** `signInWithPassword`, same accounts as the web app. Load the row from `profiles` for the role.
- **Reads (RLS already filters per user):** tables `schedule_entries`, `sections`, `courses`, `rooms`, `instructors`, `terms`, `room_requests`; views `room_status_now` and `locker_status`.
- **The mobile app does not schedule.** It covers CCTV monitoring and the accept or reject of room requests (and, if wanted, the dean's accept or reject of a schedule). Use only the calls below.
- **Room requests:** read `room_requests` (join `instructors(full_name)` and `rooms(code, name)`), filter `status = pending` for the queue. Decide with RPC `decide_room_request(p_request_id, p_approve, p_note)` (dean or admin; a note is required to reject). Instructors use `free_rooms(p_date, p_start, p_end)`, `create_room_request(p_room_id, p_date, p_start, p_end, p_reason)` and `cancel_room_request(p_request_id)`.
- **Schedule decisions:** RPC `set_schedule_status(p_term_id, p_program_id, p_to, p_note)`. Accept is `approved` then `published`; reject is `returned` with a note. `schedule_review_note(p_term_id, p_program_id)` returns the dean's latest note.
- **Live updates:** subscribe to Realtime on `access_logs`, `key_return_events`, `occupancy_events`, `schedule_entries`, `room_requests`.
- **Cameras:** read `cameras` (admin and dean only by policy; join `rooms(code, name)`). The live picture is WHEP over WebRTC: POST an SDP offer to `<gateway_url>/<stream_path>/whep` with header `Authorization: Bearer <the user's access token>` (with `flutter_webrtc`). The verdict for each room is `room_status_now.presence` (and `person_count`); do not recompute it. A phone may count people too, but that is optional: if it does, call RPC `report_occupancy(p_room_id, p_people, p_confidence)` exactly as the web page does (admin or dean; it is rate limited by the database).
- **Alerts (optional for the phone):** RPC `ops_alerts(p_hours)` (administrator or dean; other roles get no rows) returns the same alert list as the dashboard as codes and facts (`kind`, `severity`, `code`, `room_code`, `subject`, `occurred_at`, `attempts`); the wording is in `web/src/lib/alerts.ts`. The `station_status` view says whether the locker station is online.
- **Notifications (step 9):** table `notifications` (select own rows only: `id, kind, severity, title, body, link, ref, created_at, read_at`; `severity` is `high`, `medium` or `info`; `link` is a page of the web app and the phone maps it to its own pages, see `mobile/lib/core/inbox.dart`). Mark read with RPC `mark_notifications_read(p_ids)` (an empty list changes nothing, no argument or null marks all of the caller's own). Administrators and deans keep the alerts flowing by calling RPC `sweep_my_alerts()` about once a minute while the app is open (it returns how many messages it made and lets one through per school every 20 seconds). Subscribe to Realtime on `notifications` filtered by `user_id=eq.<uid>`. The phone app does this and shows a banner and a badge.
- **Capturing trials (web only):** RPC `trial_candidates(p_hours)`, `capture_trial(p_subsystem, p_source_id, p_condition, p_observer, p_notes)` and `dismiss_trial_candidates(p_subsystem, p_source_ids)`, administrator only.
- **Not for apps:** `authorize_card_at_station`, `authorize_key_request`, `record_key_return`, `record_tap_latency` and `station_heartbeat` are callable by `service_role` only. They belong to the locker firmware, not to any app. (`record_test_trial`, `delete_test_trial` and `record_solve_run` are for the web app's Evaluation page and Generate dialog. The functions in the `private` schema, including the ones the triggers and the sweep use, are not reachable by any signed-in user and must stay that way.)
- Query shapes to copy are in `web/src/pages/*.tsx` and the row types in `web/src/lib/types.ts`.
- An instructor's phone sees only their own published classes, by policy, with no client-side filtering needed.

## Where this build differs from the written proposal

The proposal (Chapters 1 and 2) was written before some choices were made. The system does these things differently; update the text or be ready to explain them:

- **Four key compartments, not nine.** The locker is one cabinet with four doors in the LRC 3rd floor (one key per room, four rooms). The proposal's Scope, Figures 2.7 and 2.9 and the "nine compartments" wording describe nine. The locale also says the SCITE building's faculty room and the CpE laboratory; the demo data uses the LRC.
- **Web and phone are two codebases.** The proposal says the mobile and web apps are built from one Flutter codebase (Figures 2.1 and 2.6). The web app is React and TypeScript; the phone app is Flutter and does only camera monitoring and the accept or reject of room requests. Its screens are not the instructor home page and schedule approval screens that Figure 2.6 describes.
- **No separate optimization or computer-vision service.** Figure 2.1 and the IPO model show a server-side optimization service and a computer-vision service on a single-board computer. Here the scheduling engine (constraint programming) runs in the browser of the person who presses Generate, and the person-counting model runs on the device that is watching the camera. The parent Raspberry Pi only relays the camera stream (MediaMTX) and checks who may watch.
- **The camera counts people, it does not only answer yes or no.** The proposal limits the vision task to binary presence. The system reports a person count and derives presence from it; it still does not identify anyone, and no footage or image leaves the watching device.
- **The response time is measured from the program receiving the card number**, not from the reader sensing the card, and it leaves out the up-to-50 ms wait before the program next looks at the reader. State this when reporting the 500 ms target.
- **The F1-score is computed as 2TP / (2TP + FP + FN)**, which equals the harmonic mean of precision and recall in the proposal's formula and also gives a value (0) in the case where precision is undefined but recall is 0. A cell with nothing to divide shows a dash, never a made-up zero.
- **Notifications arrive in the app, not on a closed phone.** Figure 2.2's notification on the administrator dashboard is the Alerts card plus a bell with a message for each new request, decision and alert. They reach a screen that is open (web or phone), and the web app can show a pop-up from a hidden tab. A phone with the app closed is not woken (that needs Firebase push; see "Notifications"), and there is no email.
